# Processes and Treatment Objectives

A process is an activity performed by equipment or by a system; a treatment
objective is the intended change that activity serves. They are separate vocabularies because
neither relation fits inside the other's hierarchy: sedimentation serves
clarification and thickening alike, and disinfection is reached by chlorination,
ozonation, ultraviolet irradiation and heat. This page shows each hierarchy once
and then the correspondence between them, in both directions.

Generated by `scripts/generate-process-outcome-map.py` from `water/processtypes.ttl`
and `water/outcomes.ttl`.

### Treatment objectives stated only on equipment

No process asserts these, by design: what a clarifier or a precipitation step is
*for* depends on where it sits and what reagent it doses, so the objective is
declared on the equipment class in `water/equipment.ttl` instead. This is the
reason the two axes are separate, and the reason `watr:TreatmentObjectiveCompletenessShape`
only checks that a stated process implies a stated treatment objective and never the
converse. Treatment objectives left out below are entailed through a subclass — nothing
asserts Nutrient Removal, but a denitrification step reaches Nitrogen Removal
under it.


- **Clarification** — Production of a clarified liquid stream by removing suspended solids from it. The objective of every clarifier, whatever the stage it serves and whatever mechanism it separates by.
- **Dechlorination** — Removal of the free and combined chlorine residual left by disinfection, before discharge or before a downstream process that the residual would damage.
- **Desalination** — Reduction of the dissolved salt content of the treated stream, whether by a membrane, an electrical or a thermal process.
- **Dewatering** — Removing water from a sludge or slurry to the point that the product is a handleable cake rather than a pumpable liquid.
- **Drying** — Removing water from biosolids beyond the cake stage, to a dry product.
- **Land Application** — Final disposition of biosolids by beneficial reuse on agricultural or reclamation land.
- **Landfill** — Final disposition of biosolids by landfilling.
- **Metals Removal** — Removal of dissolved metals from the treated stream, typically by raising pH or dosing sulfide so that they precipitate as hydroxides or sulfides and are then separated out.
- **Silica Removal** — Reduction of the dissolved silica content of the treated stream, generally to protect downstream membranes from silica scaling.
- **Softening** — Reduction of hardness by removing calcium and magnesium from the treated stream.
- **Solids Removal** — Net removal of suspended or settleable solids from the treated stream.
- **Sulfate Removal** — Reduction of the dissolved sulfate content of the treated stream, as in acid mine drainage treatment where it is precipitated as gypsum or ettringite.
- **Thickening** — Raising the solids concentration of a sludge or slurry while it remains pumpable. Distinguished from dewatering by the state of the product.
- **Turbidity Removal** — Reduction of the turbidity of the treated stream, the objective of polishing filtration.

## Compound processes

`watr:includesProcess` is a different relation from both of the above: it records
the steps a treatment train is made of, so that a system claiming the compound
process can be checked for members covering them. A compound process *comprises*
its steps; it is not a kind of them.
